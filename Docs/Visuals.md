# Visuals design

- The table's look comes from research the author did with Claude, recorded with its sources and contrast measurements in a claude.ai doc of his: https://claude.ai/artifact/9ozjdkbWXDZAy9CaH328e9. It covers the colors, the font's weight, post-processing, the table, the boards' backgrounds, the blocks' outline and the panel's style. The goal is a better look, not exact colors on screen (see the colors set aside, below).
- Colors are written in sRGB, as hexadecimal `FColor` values, the form that image editors and the research use, and converted with `FLinearColor(FColor)`, which turns them into the linear values that the Color parameter of `M_Block` (Unlit) takes.
- The colors: the table #1B1F25; the boards' backgrounds #2A3039, one color for every board and for the info panel's two backgrounds; the operators blue #88C0FF (see below); the parentheses gray #B4BBC4, apart from the other operators, so the groups show at a glance; the propositions yellow #F2CC4D; black text on every block; an outline on every block, near-black (#0D0F12), or white when the block is selected; the indicators' circles cyan #16D7D7, red #EF4444 or purple #BB86F2, with black symbols and white labels, and the statement in white (see `Docs/InfoPanel.md`); the formula's outline in the equivalence indicator's color (see below); the Submit button's outline cyan #16D7D7, the indicators' yes, while it is enabled; and the result screen's box in the boards' #2A3039, with its outline and its OK button's in the result's color, cyan #16D7D7 for a victory and bright red #FF0000 for a defeat (see `Docs/LevelResult.md`).
- The author prefers yellow for the propositions to the orange #E69F00 that the research picked, and a muted yellow, since pure yellow (#FFFF02, tried first) outshone the blue.
- Black text has a contrast ratio (WCAG) of 13.5:1 on the propositions' yellow, 10.8:1 on the parentheses' gray and 11.0:1 on the operators' blue, computed from the code's colors. The indicators' black symbols have 11.7:1 on the cyan, 5.6:1 on the red and 7.8:1 on the purple, and the info panel's white text, the indicators' labels and the statement, 13.3:1 on its backgrounds. The panel's white text has at least the 4.5:1 agreed for it: 10.2:1 on Normal, 4.8:1 on Hovered and 16.5:1 on Pressed. The result screen's white message has the info panel's 13.3:1, on the same background color, and its OK button has the panel's style.
- Each class sets its own colors in its code, as defaults (see Data rules in `CLAUDE.md`): `APropositionBlock` in its constructor; `AOperatorBlock` in `OnConstruction`, since it chooses between gray and blue by its token, which arrives through `SetData` during the deferred spawn, after the constructor; `ABlock` the black text, on its label's field in its constructor (see `Docs/Data.md`), and the outline's two colors, through `GetOutlineColor`; `UBlockBoard` the color of its background; `UInfoPanel` the colors of its backgrounds and of the statement, in its constructor; and `UIndicator` the colors of its texts, in its constructor, and its circle's color through `ChangeColor`, when told (see `Docs/InfoPanel.md`); while the rest of the panel's look and the result screen's live in their Widget Blueprints. The exception is the colors that several classes share: the indicators' yes, no and not applicable, and the defeat's, are getters of `UThisIsLogicSettings`, which the GameMode reads and hands out through each piece's `SetData`: the three answers to the exercise, which passes them to `UInfoPanel`, which hands one to each `UIndicator` on each answer it shows; the yes and the defeat's to `UResultScreen`, for its outlines (see `Docs/LevelResult.md`); and the yes to `UCommandPanel`, for the Submit button's outline, so `WBP_CommandPanel` no longer sets that color. The formula's outline gets its color from the exercise (see below). `URoundedBackgroundComponent` still has two ways to set a color (see Pending in `CLAUDE.md`).
- The operators' sky blue (#5EB8EA) was close to the indicators' cyan (#22D3EE), 13° apart in hue. The blue moved toward pure blue, with less green, and the cyan became pure cyan, with equal green and blue (#16D7D7), keeping the perceived lightness and saturation of the old one, measured in OKLCH, a color space built on perception, rather than the L of HSL, the lightness of image editors, which does not match what the eye sees: at the same HSL lightness, pure blue is much darker, and black text on it fell to 4.3:1. A pure blue at the same perceived lightness (#A3A3F4) is lavender, close to the purple, and a stronger one (#8888FF) still looked purple, so the author raised its green until it no longer did, accepting a lighter blue: #88C0FF, 212° in HSL, against the cyan's 180°. The indicators still do not rely on color alone: each circle also shows a symbol (see `Docs/InfoPanel.md`).
- The blocks' text is bold, from DejaVu Sans Condensed Bold (see `Docs/Font.md`). Each block class decides its label size through `GetLabelSize`, a virtual method of `ABlock` (Template Method): half the block's height for propositions and 0.7 of it for operators, so that the symbols fill their circles. `ABlock::Height` is protected for that: it is a `float`, so hiding it behind a ratio returned by each subclass, as `GetRowHeightRatio` does in the manager, would add methods while hiding no class (see Engineering principles in `CLAUDE.md`).
- Post-processing changed the blocks' unlit colors: the exposure (Auto Exposure Bias 1) doubled them, the filmic tone curve made them pastel, bloom drew a halo that blurred the text, Local Exposure's contrast (0.8 by default since 5.5) brightened the dark table, more where it was far from the blocks, and the vignette (0.4 by default) darkened the screen from the center, to about 60% at the corners.
- Project Settings (`DefaultEngine.ini`) turns bloom off and sets Auto Exposure Bias to 0 and Local Exposure's Highlight Contrast Scale and Shadow Contrast Scale to 1. It has no defaults for the tone curve, the gamut or the vignette, so the camera sets them in its `PostProcessSettings` (`ACameraPawn::SetPostProcess`): Tone Curve Amount 0, Expand Gamut 0 and Vignette Intensity 0, each with its `bOverride_` flag. A field left unchecked in a camera or a Post Process Volume does not turn a feature off: the engine's default applies.
- Lumen, Unreal's dynamic global illumination and reflections, which the project template turned on, is off in Project Settings (`DefaultEngine.ini`): Dynamic Global Illumination Method and Reflection Method are None (`r.DynamicGlobalIlluminationMethod=0` and `r.ReflectionMethod=0`). Nothing on the table uses light, since its materials are unlit and it has no light, and Lumen's work for orthographic cameras showed up in the log as a performance warning of the engine's own, about `r.Lumen.Ortho.OverrideMeshDFTraceDistances`. Hardware ray tracing and mesh distance fields, which served only Lumen and the path tracer, are still on (see Pending in `CLAUDE.md`).
- Anti-aliasing is TSR (`r.AntiAliasingMethod=4`). `r.MSAACount=4`, also in `DefaultEngine.ini`, does nothing, since MSAA needs forward shading.
- UMG skips post-processing, so the same color looks darker in the panel than in the world, and the panel's colors were chosen by eye.
- Set aside by the author's decision: the screen does not show exactly the colors the code sets. Measured in a screenshot, everything in the world was about 19% brighter in linear values than in the code (the table about #1E2228 instead of #1B1F25, the blue blocks in the same ratio), probably because of the exposure; not investigated.
- The table is a Plane in `Main`: the engine's 1 m plane, at scale 1, at Z −10, with no collision, named `Table`, and with `MI_Table` (#1B1F25), an instance of `M_Block`, in `Content/Table/`. It covers windows from 1:2 to 32:9. By the author's decision, it is an exception to the data rules and to `Main` holding no pieces, since it is only a mesh with a color. `Main` no longer has a DirectionalLight or a SkyAtmosphere, which unlit materials do not need. `ABackgroundTarget` still takes the presses on empty space, with its box between the blocks and the table (see `Docs/Input.md` and `Docs/Scale.md`); it may later be renamed (such as `ATable`) and take the table's mesh, replacing the box.
- Every board has a background, by the author's decision: a `URoundedBackgroundComponent` that `UBlockBoard` holds, 0.1 block height below the blocks, with no collision (see `Docs/Layout.md`). Its size is fixed: the rows' maximum width plus a margin of 0.3 block height on each side, by a number of rows that each subclass of the manager gives through `GetBackgroundRowCount` (see `Docs/BlocksManager.md`): 6 in the formula, 13 in the operator palette and 2 in the proposition palette. Its corners have a fixed radius of 0.4 block height (see `Docs/Background.md`).
- The formula's background has an outline, which sets it apart from the proposition palette's, of the same color: a second `URoundedBackgroundComponent` of the board, 0.15 block height below the blocks, with no collision. As on the blocks, the board's size is the outline's, and the background shrinks by the outline's thickness on each side, so the table's arrangement keeps its numbers; its corner radius shrinks by the thickness too, so the outline keeps its width along the corners. Each subclass of the manager gives the thickness through `GetOutlineThicknessRatio`, in block heights: 0.06 in the formula, as on the blocks (0.12 cm, about the 4 of the result screen's outline on a 1080-pixel-high window), and 0 in the palettes, whose board then builds no outline. Its color follows the equivalence indicator, cyan, red or purple: after each check, the exercise asks the info panel for the equivalence's color and hands it to the formula, which passes it to its board, so the choice among the three colors stays in the panel (see `Docs/InfoPanel.md`).
- From top to bottom, right of the operator palette, with 0.3 cm between the backgrounds: the info panel, whose top lies at 14.6 cm, where the operator palette's background starts; the proposition palette, whose background goes from 8.9 cm to 3.5 cm; and the formula, whose background goes from 3.2 cm to −15.0 cm, where the operator palette's ends (see `Docs/Scale.md`). On windows wider than 50:28, the camera cuts the table at the top and at the bottom, since it is taller than the 28 cm always shown. The operator palette's background stands 0.3 cm from the others, which the author accepts in place of the 1 cm agreed before, and the info panel's two backgrounds stand 0.3 cm apart too, rather than one panel margin (0.6 cm), which looked larger than the other gaps.
- The outline: the block's `Width` and `Height` are its outer size, the outline's, and its background is smaller by the outline's thickness on each side (0.06 block height, 0.12 cm), so the block takes the same room as before the outline and its text keeps its size. The selection only changes the outline's color, through `URoundedBackgroundComponent::ChangeColor` (see `Docs/Background.md`). The rows place the blocks by `Width`, so neighboring outlines touch, which the author accepts in place of the gap between blocks agreed before. The outline has no collision, so a press on it reaches `ABackgroundTarget` (see `Docs/Input.md`).
- The panel's look lives in `WBP_CommandPanel` (see `Docs/Data.md`). Its buttons use a Rounded Box with a fixed radius of 20 and an outline of width 0 in the fill's color: #39424C when Normal and Disabled, #667488 when Hovered and #1B1F25, the table's color, when Pressed; Slot Padding 6 on each side, and Normal and Pressed Padding 12 × 4. The panel sits 12 from the bottom of the screen and fits its content (Size To Content). Its selection buttons are disabled rather than hidden (see `Docs/Commands.md`). The row is a Horizontal Box inside a Size Box with a Width Override of 1350, and Submit, apart from the others after a slot padding of 48 on its left, has a slot that fills the rest of the row (Size Fill), aligned to the right, so it stays at the row's right edge whatever the other buttons' widths. Submit adds a cyan outline of width 3 to its Normal, Hovered and Pressed states, and none when disabled, so it lights up with the equivalence indicator; its color comes from the code (see above), and its width from the Widget Blueprint. The Size Box's width and the Border's content padding (0, 2, 27, 2) were set by eye so that Restart lines up with the operator palette and Submit with the formula's outline on the author's usual window; on other windows they do not (see Pending in `CLAUDE.md`).
- The result screen's look lives in `WBP_ResultScreen`: a box in the middle of the screen, 1000 × 500, to look like a Windows alert box, a Border with a Rounded Box of fixed radius 20 in the boards' color and an outline of width 4, in the result's color, which sets it apart from the formula's background beneath, of the same color. The message, at size 48, in two centered lines, fills the space above the OK button, which has the panel's buttons' style with its text also at 48 and an outline of width 2 in the result's color (see `Docs/LevelResult.md`).
- Rejected: the orange #E69F00 that the research picked for the propositions (the author prefers yellow); pure yellow #FFFF02 (it outshone the blue); white text on the blocks (1.5:1 to 1.9:1 on their colors); the engine's default post-processing (it changed the unlit colors, as above); a Post Process Volume in `Main` (values in an asset, against the data rules; it was used only before knowing how to set them in code); a darker sheet behind the formula (#232830) and backgrounds that grow with the rows (both dropped by the author while trying the look); keeping the HSL lightness when moving the blue toward pure blue (it darkened the blue and the black text's contrast, as above); a pure blue, with equal red and green, for the operators (at the old lightness it was lavender, close to the purple, and stronger it still looked purple); gray (#8B949E) for an equivalence that does not apply (the author preferred purple); a darker purple (#A960EE, with the red's perceived lightness, which fought with the black symbol); a single red for the indicators' no and the defeat (UMG skips post-processing, so the indicators' red looked dull on the result screen, and the screen's red outshone the black symbols in the indicators; see `Docs/LevelResult.md`); the formula's outline growing the board past its old size (every hand-computed place on the table would change).
