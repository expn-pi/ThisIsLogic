# Info panel design

- `UInfoPanel` shows the level's statement and the two indicators of the game design (see Game design in `CLAUDE.md`). It is a scene component of `AExercise`, which creates it in its constructor and attaches it to its root, as the manager does with its board (see `Docs/BlocksManager.md`). The author sees the panel as an extension of the exercise: its place is the exercise's, which the GameMode decides (see `Docs/Assembly.md`), so the GameMode never knows its class. In Unreal, a component, rather than a separate actor, is the usual way to extend an actor, and it lives and dies with it.
- The panel has two backgrounds side by side, with the same height: the statement's on the left and the indicators' on the right, one for both indicators. The statement and the indicators stay in one class: the statement's part is too small for a class of its own, and the panel moves on the table as a whole.
- Each indicator is a `UIndicator`, a scene component that the panel creates twice in its constructor: a colored circle with a symbol, followed by a label. It knows nothing of validity or equivalence: the panel gives it its label's text and the assets (`SetData`), its size (`Build`) and, at each `Show`, the color and the symbol to show, so both indicators share one class, with no subclasses. The names of its own subobjects (`Circle`, `Symbol`, `Label`) only need to be unique within each indicator, their outer.
- The exercise computes and the panel shows (Tell, Don't Ask). The exercise keeps the solution's tree and is the only class that calls the logic core (see `Docs/Exercise.md`), and the win condition uses the same answers: the equivalence also enables the panel's Submit (see `Docs/LevelResult.md`). After each change of the formula, and also at the start, after Restart and after an Undo, which send no change event, it passes two answers to `Show`: whether the formula is well formed and whether it is equivalent to the solution. The second only counts when the first is true, like the first two values of the formula's selection event (see `Docs/Assembly.md`). Right after, the exercise asks the panel for the equivalence's color (`GetEquivalenceColor`, public) and hands it to the formula, whose outline shows it (see `Docs/Visuals.md`): the panel decides the colors, so the choice among the three stays in one class, and the formula only receives a color, with no notion of equivalence.
- The indicators are in two rows: validity above ("Well-formed") and equivalence below ("Equivalent"). The circles answer cyan (#16D7D7) for yes and red (#EF4444) for no; the equivalence's circle is purple (#BB86F2) when the formula is not well formed, since the question does not apply. The three colors come from `UThisIsLogicSettings`, since the result screen and the panel's Submit share the yes (see `Docs/Visuals.md` and `Docs/Data.md`), and the panel keeps them, as it keeps the statement.
- The symbols keep the indicators from relying on color alone, which mattered most while the operators' blue was close to the cyan (see `Docs/Visuals.md`): ✔ or ✗ for validity, and ≡, ≢ or ∅ for equivalence, ∅ when the question does not apply. ≡ and ≢ come from the notation (see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`), since they are logic notation, and some courses write the biconditional itself as ≡: a style where ≡ is an operator would choose other symbols there. ✔, ✗ and ∅ are interface marks that do not depend on the course, so they are fixed in `UInfoPanel`, as named local variables in the methods that use them. Until the player chooses the notation (4.1), the panel keeps its own, with its defaults, as each factory does.
- The symbols are black, at 0.7 of the circle's diameter, like the operators' symbols; the labels and the statement are white, at 0.6 of the diameter, since they lie on the dark backgrounds. All of them use the blocks' font, which includes the symbols, and the unlit text material `M_Text` (see `Docs/Font.md`). The font holds only the printable ASCII characters and the symbols, so the statements are written in English, like the sentences.
- The statement is the level's `Statement` column. The GameMode reads it and passes it to the exercise together with the assets, and the exercise passes it to the panel, which keeps it until `BeginPlay`. There, the statement is centered in its background, horizontally and vertically, and broken into lines that fit the background minus its margins: the words enter the current line one by one, each new line measured with the statement's own label (`GetTextLocalSize`, whose Y is the width), and a line that the next word would overflow ends with `<br>`, the line separator that `UTextRenderComponent` accepts; a word too wide on its own still takes a line. Measuring with the label takes the real font and size into account, and it runs once per level. If the wrapped text is taller than its room (the Z of `GetTextLocalSize`), a `check` stops the game: the levels are written by their author, so a statement too long is an error to fix, as in `Docs/Levels.md`. About three lines of about 50 characters fit.
- The panel is anchored at the right edge of the indicators' background, at the height of its center, so the GameMode only aligns that edge with the formula's background (see `Docs/Scale.md`). A label's width is known only once the font arrives, so the panel is laid out in `BeginPlay`: each indicator builds itself from the circles' diameter, anchored at its left edge, with its circle and then its label, and returns its width; the indicators' background takes the wider one plus its margins, with the indicators at its left side; and the statement's background takes the rest of the panel's width, 0.3 cm away to the left (0.15 of the diameter, a local variable of `BuildStatement`), like the gaps between the boards; the statement, centered in its background, follows its width. Sizes derive from the circles' diameter, 2 cm like the blocks' height, with the boards' ratios for the backgrounds' margin (0.3), corner radius (0.4) and depth (0.1), so they look like boards; the gaps between a circle and its label (0.3) and between the two rows (0.1) are provisional. The panel's height, two circles, the gap between the rows and the margins, is 5.4 cm, the room at the top of the table, above the proposition palette; its width is 41.2 cm, the formula's background (see Pending in `CLAUDE.md`). Nothing in the panel has collision, so a press on it reaches the background target (see `Docs/Input.md`).
- Only three sizes are constants of the class: the circles' diameter, the backgrounds' margin, used in three places, and the panel's width. Every other size is a local variable of the method that uses it, derived from the diameter, which the indicators receive as a parameter of their `Build`. Each size is computed once per level, so constants bought nothing and made the methods harder to read. Since `CreateDefaultSubobject` passes no parameters to the constructor, the constructors set only what does not depend on size (the parts, their attachments, the text's rotation, alignment and color), and the rest is applied in `Build`, called from the panel's `BeginPlay`, as with `URoundedBackgroundComponent`.
- The statement, the assets (the base material, the font and the text material) and the answers' colors come from the settings and the level through the GameMode, which passes them to the exercise, which passes them to the panel, which passes the assets to the indicators, by the author's decision. They come together, once, so a single method carries them at each step, as `ABlock::SetData` does.
- Rejected: the panel as a subclass of `AAbstractBlocksManager` (its board creates blocks only from tokens, each with its color decided once at creation, while an indicator has no token and changes color and symbol during the game; and it would inherit gestures it does not use, the Refused Bequest); the panel computing validity and equivalence, with code moved from the exercise (the exercise would stop being the only class that calls the logic core, and the solution's tree, which the win condition needs, is the exercise's); the panel listening to the formula's change event (it has a single listener, the exercise, and the start, Restart and Undo send no event); the GameMode creating the panel and handing it to the exercise, so that it decides its place (rejected by the author: the panel is an extension of the exercise, placed relative to it, and the GameMode need not know its class); a separate actor that the exercise spawns and attaches (a component is the usual way to extend an actor); ≡ and ≢ as token kinds (they are not pieces of a formula: they compare two); one setter per asset (they come together, once); rounded squares for the colored markers and one background per indicator, as in the author's first mockup (the author prefers circles and a single background); the first layout, `UIndicators`, with the indicators side by side, each label before its circle and followed by a colon (the author's second mockup puts each circle before its label, the indicators in two rows and the statement beside them); `UIndicators` keeping a circle, a symbol and a label for each indicator, with nearly the same methods written twice; a subclass of `UIndicator` for each indicator (they differ only in the color and the symbol to show, which the panel decides); one constant of the class for each size derived from the diameter (most were used once); the statement in a class of its own, beside the indicators' (too small for a class, and the panel moves as a whole); creating the statement's label in `SetData` (`CreateDefaultSubobject` only works inside a constructor, and later it stops the game with "No object initializer found during construction"; components created after construction need `NewObject`, `AttachToComponent` and `RegisterComponent`, worth it only when their number varies); line breaks written by hand in the levels (tied to the font, the sizes and the language, so every change would mean revising every level); shrinking the font until the statement fits (on its own, a long statement becomes tiny; it may come later, as a step before the `check`); a UMG widget for the statement, which wraps by itself (two interface systems in one panel, and a second font, a runtime one; see `Docs/Palette.md`); the statement aligned to the right (each line would start at a different place, which slows reading) or to the left and the top (better for reading long statements, but most statements take one or two lines, which left the background's right side empty); ✓ for a well-formed formula (about a third of the ink of ≡, so it looked faint; ✔ has about 1.7 times as much); ✘ for an ill-formed one (almost twice the ink of ✔, which unbalanced the pair, while ✗ is close to ✔); an empty gray circle when equivalence does not apply (it read as a missing symbol); a light on the table, so that the default text material would show the labels white (it did, but it changed the table's look; see `Docs/Font.md`); the formula listening to the answers and choosing its outline's color itself (the choice among three colors would be written twice); the gap between the statement's and the indicators' backgrounds as one margin (0.6 cm, twice the gaps between the boards).
