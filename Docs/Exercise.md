# Exercise design

- `AExercise` is the problem of a level, at the top of the problem's level of abstraction (see Engineering principles in `CLAUDE.md`): it keeps its starting tokens, the solution's tree and the undo history, and talks to the formula only in tokens and block states. It never touches a block, a factory or a position on the table; the GameMode creates it and gives it the formula and the level's starting formula and solution, as texts (see `Docs/Assembly.md`), and a `check` stops an exercise started without a formula. `SetFormulasTexts` analyzes both texts right away, through `AnalyzeFormula`, and builds their trees, through `ParseLevelFormula`; these are its bridges to the logic core. It is the only class that calls the logic core, which keeps the conversions between engine types and standard types in one place. `ConnectFormula` binds the formula's change event. The start of the level and Restart send the starting tokens to the formula (`SetTokens`), and Undo sends it a snapshot of the blocks' states (`SetStates`). Later the exercise will decide the result (3.1), from the solution's tree. The selection stays in `AFormula`, which keeps what the blocks mean (and later the tree), while `UBlockBoard` keeps the blocks themselves.
- `SetFormulasTexts` stops the game at a `check` when one of the level's formulas is not well formed or the solution is not equivalent to the starting formula, since either is a typo of the level's author (see `Docs/Levels.md`). The starting formula's tree exists only for that check; the exercise keeps the solution's, and compares each change of the player's formula with it. Both references give the same answer, since the check guarantees that they are equivalent, and the solution, being smaller, makes each row of the truth table cheaper; the starting formula is what the player simplifies, not the reference for each move. The two texts come through one setter, since the check needs both.
- When the formula reports a change, besides saving the state, the exercise builds the tree of the player's formula from its tokens (`AFormula::GetTokens`) and, until the indicators exist (2.4), logs whether the formula is well formed and, if so, whether it is equivalent to the solution (`LogIndicators`).
- Restart and Undo are commands of `AExercise`, since the starting tokens and the history of the problem's states are its own; the formula only rebuilds the blocks it is given. The history lives in `FUndoHistory`, a plain class held as a member, which only keeps the snapshots and gives back the previous one (the caretaker of the Memento pattern); the exercise decides when to save (at the start, after Restart and when the formula reports a change) and how to restore (see `Docs/Commands.md`). The change event carries no data: the exercise asks the formula for the blocks' states. Restart and Undo are public, since the GameMode binds the panel's buttons straight to them.
- The name comes from the exercises of a logic course, which the game imitates: each has a statement, a formula to simplify, tools to work on it and a result.
- Rejected names: `AAdministrator` (it reads as system administration); `ATeacher` (the exercise is what the student works on, not who sets it up; setting up the table is the GameMode's role); `APuzzle` (generic); `Level` for a field with the level's row (in Unreal, a level is a map, and every actor has a `GetLevel()` that returns its map); `SetFormulas` for the setter of the level's texts (one letter away from `SetFormula`, which takes the formula actor).
- Rejected: Undo as a command of `AFormula` (the history and the starting tokens belong to the problem, and the formula only rebuilds the blocks it is given); the history kept in fields and methods of `AExercise` itself (a class of its own keeps one responsibility, and the exercise only decides when to save and how to restore); the exercise keeping a factory and creating the blocks for the formula (the problem's level would handle actors, sizes and the notation; see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`); the exercise reading its level, keeping the sentences or receiving tokens already analyzed (see the rejected alternatives in `Docs/Assembly.md`); comparing each change with the starting formula's tree (the first version: the solution gives the same answer at a lower cost, and the starting formula is what the player simplifies); one setter per text, `SetStartingFormula` and `SetSolution` (the check needs both texts, so the second setter would depend on the first having been called).
