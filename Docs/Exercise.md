# Exercise design

- `AExercise` is the problem of a level, at the top of the problem's level of abstraction (see Engineering principles in `CLAUDE.md`): it loads its level, keeps its starting tokens, its sentences and the undo history, and talks to the formula only in tokens and block states. It never touches a block, a factory or a position on the table; the GameMode creates it and gives it the formula (see `Docs/Assembly.md`), and a `check` stops an exercise started without one. `LoadFormula` reads the level once: the starting tokens, through `AnalyzeFormula`, its bridge to the logic core, and the sentences. `ConnectFormula` then hands the sentences to the formula, once, through `SetSentences`. The start of the level and Restart send the starting tokens to the formula (`SetTokens`), and Undo sends it a snapshot of the blocks' states (`SetStates`); the starting tokens stay kept for the equivalence indicator. For now the level is the first row of the levels' DataTable (see `Docs/Levels.md`). Later the exercise will decide the result. The selection stays in `AFormula`, which keeps what the blocks mean (and later the tree), while `UBlockBoard` keeps the blocks themselves.
- The exercise keeps a copy of its level's row rather than the pointer that `FindRow` returns: that pointer points into the asset, which the engine may unload once nothing holds it, and the soft reference in the settings does not hold it.
- Restart and Undo are commands of `AExercise`, since the starting tokens and the history of the problem's states are its own; the formula only rebuilds the blocks it is given. The history lives in `FUndoHistory`, a plain class held as a member, which only keeps the snapshots and gives back the previous one (the caretaker of the Memento pattern); the exercise decides when to save (at the start, after Restart and when the formula reports a change) and how to restore (see `Docs/Commands.md`). The change event carries no data: the exercise asks the formula for the blocks' states. Restart and Undo are public, since the GameMode binds the panel's buttons straight to them.
- The name comes from the exercises of a logic course, which the game imitates: each has a statement, a formula to simplify, tools to work on it and a result.
- Rejected names: `AAdministrator` (it reads as system administration); `ATeacher` (the exercise is what the student works on, not who sets it up; setting up the table is the GameMode's role); `APuzzle` (generic); `Level` for the field with the level's row (in Unreal, a level is a map, and every actor has a `GetLevel()` that returns its map).
- Rejected: Undo as a command of `AFormula` (the history and the starting tokens belong to the problem, and the formula only rebuilds the blocks it is given); the history kept in fields and methods of `AExercise` itself (a class of its own keeps one responsibility, and the exercise only decides when to save and how to restore); the exercise keeping a factory and creating the blocks for the formula (the problem's level would handle actors, sizes and the notation; see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`).
