# Layout design

- `FBlockBoard` keeps the order of the blocks and lays them out in rows; `AFormula` holds one and keeps what the blocks mean (the selection, and later the tree). The board moves blocks only when its owner asks, and the owner decides what a gesture means (moving now; copying or removing later), so the palette may reuse the board (separation of mechanism and policy). It is a `USTRUCT` held in a `UPROPERTY`, rather than a plain class, because the garbage collector sees the blocks only through reflected properties.
- `Blocks` is the only source of the order. Rows and positions are always derived from it, never adjusted one block at a time.
- Rows break like the lines of a text (greedy line breaking): each block joins the last row if it fits within the maximum width, or else starts a new row, so no block passes the right edge. A row always starts with a block, so a block wider than the maximum stays alone in its row and is the one exception. Rows are recomputed from the order whenever it or a width changes, so blocks move between rows when a proposition is minimized or maximized, like words in a paragraph.
- The rows fill an area as wide as the maximum width (see `Docs/Scale.md`), centered on the formula, and all start at its left edge, like a paragraph: a short formula is not centered, but nothing shifts sideways when the rows change during a drag, and the area is the formula's area of `Docs/Commands.md`. The first row passes through the formula's position and new rows appear below it, so a new row never moves the others. Rows are half a block's height apart.
- `FBlockRow` is a range of the board's list (its first and last index), with its width, the point where it starts and the maximum width; it keeps no blocks. Its methods that look at blocks receive the list as a `const` parameter, and changing the order stays in the board, whose data it is. It has no getters or setters: its constructor builds it with its first block, and it grows only through its own methods.
- Blocks enter through `AddBlock` (one) or `AddBlocks` (several at once, such as the initial ones); the formula registers each block (owner and events) before handing it to the board. Every board method that changes the blocks leaves the rows laid out.
- While dragging, the block follows the pointer in both directions, held inside the formula's area: across, within the maximum width; down, between the first and the last row. Leaving the area waits for the palette, since what a drop outside it means (removing on the palette, putting back elsewhere; see `Docs/Commands.md`) depends on it.
- The row under the dragged block's center decides where it goes. In its own row, it swaps with a neighbor when its edge facing that neighbor passes the neighbor's center (it covers half of it); only the neighbors in that row are checked, and only that row is laid out again after each swap, since a swap inside a row changes neither its blocks nor its width. In another row, it enters right before the first block of that row whose center is to the right of its own center, or at the row's end, and the rows are recomputed; if it does not fit there, it starts the next row, together with the blocks that followed it.
- This is stable: when the block does not fit and goes down, the blocks after it go down too, so every block left in the row under the pointer is to its left, and the next frame finds the same place, which changes nothing and recomputes nothing. In every case, the dragged block is put back under the cursor in the same frame.
- Rejected: attaching each block to its left neighbor (dragging would carry the blocks on its right, and the order would live in two places); swapping when the center passes the neighbor's center (a wide block cannot pass a narrow one at the end of a row) or the neighbor's edge (blocks of different widths swap back and forth every frame); the swap rule for entering another row (it compares the block with its neighbors in the order, and a block arriving from another row has none there yet); centering each row or the set of rows (the rows would shift sideways when they change during a drag); rows centered vertically on the formula (a new row would move the others); free dragging before the palette exists (exceptions for drops outside the area, with no way to test them yet); getters and setters for every field of `FBlockRow` (the same data, with more code); `FBlockRow` keeping a pointer to the list (a copy of the board would leave its rows pointing at the old list); a public `AddBlock` that leaves the layout to its caller (called alone, it leaves the block out of place); only `AddBlock`, called once per block (the initial blocks arrive together); `AddBlock` calling `AddBlocks` with a one-block list (harder to read than two methods that share private ones).
