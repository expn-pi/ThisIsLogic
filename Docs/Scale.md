# Scale design

- One Unreal unit is one centimeter, and the game is built at the size of a real board game: blocks are 2 cm tall, like game pieces, and the camera sees 50 cm of the table from 50 cm above it. Meshes built in code have their real size, never another size corrected by a scale on the component or the actor, so that meshes from different sources fit together in one scene. With an orthographic camera and unlit materials the scale changes nothing on screen (only the ratio of the block's size to `OrthoWidth` does); it is kept right anyway.
- `AExercise` decides the height of the blocks (`BlockHeight`), the one value the gameplay's scale depends on, and passes it down: to `AFormula` through `SetBlockHeight`, and to `FBlockFactory` through its constructor, which gives it to each block with `SetHeight` during the deferred spawn, before `OnConstruction` (see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`).
- `ABlock` derives its sizes from its height through ratios kept as constants, in `ApplyHeight`, called by `OnConstruction`: the text size, the text margin, the outline thickness, and the small heights that put the label above the background and the outline below it. A `check` stops a block spawned without a height, which would otherwise be invisible. `AFormula` derives from the height how high a dragged block rises (`DragHeight`), so that it passes over the others.
- Heights along Z only decide what is drawn in front: the camera looks straight down, so they never show. The label and the outline sit 1/20 of the block's height from its background, which leaves room against z-fighting (flicker between surfaces at almost the same depth).
- The background target sits 5 cm below the exercise, a fixed value rather than one derived from the block's height. With 0.5 cm between the blocks and the top of its box, clicks hit the background instead of the blocks; with 5 cm they hit the blocks. Tests ruled out the camera (moving the background away fixed the clicks, which a ray starting below the blocks would not allow) and the blocks' collision (2 cm blocks were hit once the background moved away). The cause is probably a tolerance of the physics (Chaos) at the millimeter scale, so the gap depends on the physics, not on the size of the pieces. Its box is 2 m × 2 m, larger than the view, and 2 mm thick.
- The camera belongs to the game mode, not to the exercise, and does not depend on the block's height: how much of the table the player sees is a separate decision. Its height is realistic, although an orthographic camera does not need it; the near and far planes are left to the engine (Auto Calculate Ortho Planes).
- The exercise sits at the origin of the `Main` map.
- Rejected: building a mesh at another size and scaling its component (a mesh at the wrong size; `Docs/Background.md` also rejects scaling the component, which stretches the corners and the label); a global scale constant included everywhere (coupling among classes that do not know each other); the background's depth derived from the block's height (smaller blocks would break the clicks again); the block's height set in the Details panel (the owner decides it); the camera's view derived from the block's height (changing the blocks' size would change nothing on screen).
