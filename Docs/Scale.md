# Scale design

- One Unreal unit is one centimeter, and the game is built at the size of a real board game: blocks are 2 cm tall, like game pieces, and the camera sees at least 50 × 28 cm of the table from 50 cm above it. Meshes built in code have their real size, never another size corrected by a scale on the component or the actor, so that meshes from different sources fit together in one scene. With an orthographic camera and unlit materials the scale changes nothing on screen (only the ratio of the block's size to `OrthoWidth` does); it is kept right anyway.
- The blocks' height is one constant of `AAbstractBlocksManager`, the base of the formula and the palettes (`BlockHeight`, 2 cm), the value their sizes depend on (see `Docs/BlocksManager.md`). The base passes it down: to its `FBlockBoard` through `SetData`, together with the distance between rows and the maximum width (see `Docs/Layout.md`), and to its `FBlockFactory` through its constructor, which gives it to each block during the deferred spawn, before `OnConstruction` (see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`). A single height guarantees that a palette item looks like the block it inserts, and the factory is built during the base's construction, when a virtual method cannot reach the subclass yet; each subclass decides only the distance between its rows. Once the board's refactor (1.24) builds the factory later, the height can be asked of the subclass too. The height belongs to the blocks' level of abstraction, so `AExercise`, which speaks in tokens, never sees it.
- The GameMode arranges the table (see `Docs/Assembly.md`): it decides where each piece goes and the maximum width of the formula's rows (`MaxRowWidth`, 40 cm), which it passes through `SetMaxRowWidth`, a method of the formula's base. The formula sits at the origin, and the operator palette on its left; these positions are first values set by eye, to be revisited with the proposition palette. The formula's width depends on how much of the table the camera always shows (50 cm across, leaving 5 cm on each side, where the operator palette fits on the left), not on the blocks' height (see `Docs/Layout.md`); a palette's width comes from its own blocks instead, and the palette passes it through the same method (see `Docs/Palette.md`).
- `ABlock` derives its sizes from its height through ratios kept as constants, in `ApplyHeight`, called by `OnConstruction`: the text size, the text margin, the outline thickness, and the small heights that put the label above the background and the outline below it. A `check` stops a block spawned without a height, which would otherwise be invisible. `FBlockBoard` derives from the height how high a dragged block rises (`DragHeight`), so that it passes over the others, and how far apart its rows are (`RowHeight`), with the ratio its owner gives (see `Docs/Layout.md`).
- Heights along Z only decide what is drawn in front: the camera looks straight down, so they never show. The label and the outline sit 1/20 of the block's height from its background, which leaves room against z-fighting (flicker between surfaces at almost the same depth).
- The background target sits 5 cm below the table's plane, where the formula and the palettes lie (`BackgroundDepth`, in the GameMode), a fixed value rather than one derived from the block's height. With 0.5 cm between the blocks and the top of its box, clicks hit the background instead of the blocks; with 5 cm they hit the blocks. Tests ruled out the camera (moving the background away fixed the clicks, which a ray starting below the blocks would not allow) and the blocks' collision (2 cm blocks were hit once the background moved away). The cause is probably a tolerance of the physics (Chaos) at the millimeter scale, so the gap depends on the physics, not on the size of the pieces. Its box is 2 m × 2 m, larger than the view, and 2 mm thick.
- The camera belongs to the GameMode, as its default pawn, and does not depend on the blocks' height: how much of the table the player sees is a separate decision. Its height is realistic, although an orthographic camera does not need it; the near and far planes are left to the engine (Auto Calculate Ortho Planes).
- The camera always shows at least a 50 × 28 cm rectangle of the table (16:9), on windows of any shape: `OrthoWidth`, the visible width, is the larger of the rectangle's width and its height times the window's aspect ratio. On a wider window, more table shows at the sides, and the blocks follow the window's height; on a narrower one, more table shows above and below, and the blocks follow its width. UMG scales the panel by the window's shortest side (the engine's default, which the project keeps), so on wide windows and on portrait ones the blocks keep their size relative to the panel's buttons.
- The camera overrides its axis constraint with `AspectRatio_MaintainXFOV`, so `OrthoWidth` is always the visible width: since 5.3, the engine's default for cameras keeps the vertical axis (Maintain Y FOV), and how 5.8 applies it to an orthographic camera was not confirmed. It recomputes when the window changes size, through `FViewport::ViewportResizedEvent`, a single event for every viewport, the editor's included, so it ignores which viewport changed and always reads the game's size. A size of zero, reported at the start of a standalone game, is skipped until the event brings the real one.
- Rejected: building a mesh at another size and scaling its component (a mesh at the wrong size; `Docs/Background.md` also rejects scaling the component, which stretches the corners and the label); a global scale constant included everywhere (coupling among classes that do not know each other); the background's depth derived from the block's height (smaller blocks would break the clicks again); the block's height set in the Details panel (the code decides it, in `AAbstractBlocksManager`); the camera's view derived from the block's height (changing the blocks' size would change nothing on screen); a fixed `OrthoWidth` (the visible height changed with the window's shape: wide windows cut the table above and below, and the blocks changed size relative to the panel's buttons); the blocks' height and the formula's maximum width decided by `AExercise` (the exercise speaks only in tokens, and the table's arrangement belongs to the GameMode).
