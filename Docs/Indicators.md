# Indicators design

- `UIndicators` shows the two indicators of the game design (see Game design in `CLAUDE.md`). It is a scene component of `AExercise`, which creates it in its constructor and attaches it to its root, as the manager does with its board (see `Docs/BlocksManager.md`). The author sees the indicators as an extension of the exercise: their place is the exercise's, which the GameMode decides (see `Docs/Assembly.md`), so the GameMode never knows their class. In Unreal, a component, rather than a separate actor, is the usual way to extend an actor, and it lives and dies with it.
- The exercise computes and the indicators show (Tell, Don't Ask). The exercise keeps the solution's tree and is the only class that calls the logic core (see `Docs/Exercise.md`), and the win condition uses the same answers: the equivalence also enables the panel's Submit (see `Docs/LevelResult.md`). After each change of the formula, and also at the start, after Restart and after an Undo, which send no change event, it passes two answers to `Show`: whether the formula is well formed and whether it is equivalent to the solution. The second only counts when the first is true, like the two values of the formula's selection event (see `Docs/Assembly.md`).
- Each indicator is a label followed by a colored circle with a symbol, all on a single background: validity on the left ("Well-formed:") and equivalence on the right ("Equivalent:"), with a colon after each label, which helps reading. The circles answer cyan (#22D3EE) for yes and red (#EF4444) for no; the equivalence's circle is gray (#8B949E) when the formula is not well formed, since the question does not apply. The colors are provisional.
- The symbols keep the indicators from relying on color alone, which matters since the operators' blue is close to the cyan (see `Docs/Visuals.md`): ✔ or ✗ for validity, and ≡, ≢ or ∅ for equivalence, ∅ when the question does not apply. ≡ and ≢ come from the notation (see `Docs/BlockCreation.md`), since they are logic notation, and some courses write the biconditional itself as ≡: a style where ≡ is an operator would choose other symbols there. ✔, ✗ and ∅ are interface marks that do not depend on the course, so they are fixed in `UIndicators`. Until the player chooses the notation (4.1), the indicators keep their own, with its defaults, as each factory does.
- The symbols are black, at 0.7 of the circle's diameter, like the operators' symbols, and the labels white, at 0.6 of the diameter, since they lie on the dark background. All of them use the blocks' font, which includes the symbols, and the unlit text material `M_Text` (see `Docs/Font.md`).
- The background is anchored at its right edge, at the height of its center, so it grows to the left with the labels, and the GameMode only aligns that edge with the formula's background (see `Docs/Scale.md`). A label's width is known only once the font arrives, so the indicators are laid out in `BeginPlay`, from right to left, each label before its circle, and the background is built with the width that results. Sizes derive from the circles' diameter, 2 cm like the blocks' height, with the boards' ratios for the background's margin (0.3), corner radius (0.4) and depth (0.1), so it looks like a board's; the gaps between a label and its circle (0.3) and between the two indicators (0.6) are provisional. Nothing in the indicators has collision, so a press on them reaches the background target (see `Docs/Input.md`).
- The assets (the base material, the font and the text material) come from the settings through the GameMode, which passes them to the exercise, which passes them to the indicators, by the author's decision. They come together, once, so a single method carries them at each step, as `ABlock::SetData` does.
- Rejected: `UIndicators` as a subclass of `AAbstractBlocksManager` (its board creates blocks only from tokens, each with its color decided once at creation, while an indicator has no token and changes color and symbol during the game; and it would inherit gestures it does not use, the Refused Bequest); the indicators computing validity and equivalence, with code moved from the exercise (the exercise would stop being the only class that calls the logic core, and the solution's tree, which the win condition needs, is the exercise's); the indicators listening to the formula's change event (it has a single listener, the exercise, and the start, Restart and Undo send no event); the GameMode creating the indicators and handing them to the exercise, so that it decides their place (rejected by the author: they are an extension of the exercise, placed relative to it, and the GameMode need not know their class); a separate actor that the exercise spawns and attaches (a component is the usual way to extend an actor); ≡ and ≢ as token kinds (they are not pieces of a formula: they compare two); one setter per asset (they come together, once); rounded squares for the colored markers and one background per indicator, as in the author's first mockup (the author prefers circles and a single background); each label after its circle, as in the mockup (the author prefers it before); ✓ for a well-formed formula (about a third of the ink of ≡, so it looked faint; ✔ has about 1.7 times as much); ✘ for an ill-formed one (almost twice the ink of ✔, which unbalanced the pair, while ✗ is close to ✔); an empty gray circle when equivalence does not apply (it read as a missing symbol); a light on the table, so that the default text material would show the labels white (it did, but it changed the table's look; see `Docs/Font.md`).
